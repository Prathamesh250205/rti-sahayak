How long does a PIO have to reply to my application?

Under the Right to Information Act, a Public Information Officer (PIO) is generally required to provide the requested information within 30 days of receiving your application [S. 7(1)]. This timeframe begins from the date the PIO actually receives the request, not the date you mailed it.

If your request concerns the life or liberty of a person, the timeline is strictly compressed. In such critical cases, the PIO must respond within 48 hours of receipt [S. 7(1)]. Additionally, if the information requested involves payment of further fees representing the cost of providing the information, the period between dispatching the intimation for further fees and payment of fees is excluded from the 30-day calculation [S. 7(3)(a)].

Section 7(1)

"...the Central Public Information Officer or State Public Information Officer, as the case may be, on receipt of a request under section 6 shall, as expeditiously as possible, and in any case within thirty days of the receipt of the request, either provide the information on payment of such fee as may be prescribed or reject the request for any of the reasons specified in sections 8 and 9:"

Failure to provide the information within these stipulated timelines is deemed as a refusal of the request [S. 7(2)]. In such instances, the applicant is entitled to file a First Appeal
